# Q364 source and priority assessment

**Verdict.** The handoff theorem matches Chen’s open corner. Moderate confidence from a bounded search that it was unresolved at this scope. No earlier treatment of full radius two with at most three voters per bundle or of the same disjoint five-vertex path restriction was located. The known larger-radius hardness remains credited.

## Original quantifiers

Jiehua Chen, *Exploiting Structure in Computationally Hard Voting Problems*, dissertation defended 18 December 2015 and issued by Universitätsverlag der TU Berlin in 2016, volume 6 of *Foundations of Computing*. Consult Definition 6.3 at printed p. 111 (PDF p. 139), the central problem at printed p. 113, Tables 6.1 and §6.6, and the first paragraph of open cases in §6.8 at printed p. 149 (PDF p. 177). The official PDF was downloaded and the relevant pages read. The institutional record is DOI 10.14279/depositonce-5113. [DepositOnce record](https://doi.org/10.14279/depositonce-5113) · [Open question](https://depositonce.tu-berlin.de/bitstreams/01ad4a50-8834-4ecd-9607-88785af545eb/download#page=177).

- The election has explicit registered and unregistered voter identities with strict rankings. A leader’s bundle contains all and only unregistered voters at distance **at most** two adjacent swaps, including the leader. The radius is fixed at two and bundles may have fewer than three voters. The cap counts the leader. The union counts a selected voter once, even when multiple selected leaders cover that voter.
- The question is existential over leader sets of cardinality **at most** the input integer k, with plurality co-winner status permitting ties. The constant three constrains bundle size b. It does not constrain k. For each fixed k, enumerating all leader subsets of size at most k is polynomial. The reduction uses k equal to the number of triples and does not prove parameterized hardness in k. [Pinned catalog wording](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/game_theory/social-choice-voting-theory.md#q364).
- The theorem permits arbitrarily many candidates, even though there are only five voters per component. Membership computes Kendall distance directly and checks the union and scores. The 3DM premise uses Karp’s perfect matching problem with equally sized parts, with trivial instances outside the reduction’s positive-size and nonisolated-X assumptions handled separately. [Karp 1972, problem 17](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9).

## Nearby results

- Chen proves linear-time solvability for full-d bundles with b at most two (Theorem 6.20), NP-completeness at b=3 for each fixed d≥3 (Theorem 6.21), and NP-completeness at b=4 for each fixed d≥1 (Theorem 6.22). Her size-two NP-hardness for anonymous functions (Theorem 6.13) has a weaker bundle restriction, as does the related leader-budget FPT result. The known small-bundle results do not supply hardness for a fixed constant leader budget. [Dissertation, §6.6](https://depositonce.tu-berlin.de/bitstreams/01ad4a50-8834-4ecd-9607-88785af545eb/download#page=152).
- Laurent Bulteau, Jiehua Chen, Piotr Faliszewski, Rolf Niedermeier and Nimrod Talmon, *Combinatorial Voter Control in Elections*, Theoret. Comput. Sci. 589 (2015), 99–120, DOI 10.1016/j.tcs.2015.04.023. The journal text’s Theorems 6–8 are the full-radius size-two algorithm, size-three hardness with radius at least three, and radius-one hardness with size four, respectively. The corresponding results are presented in the dissertation with the fixed-radius quantifiers above. The [2014 precursor](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.6859) uses different theorem numbers and phrases its size-three theorem for the general full-d class. Its proof uses radius three. It must not be cited as a proof for radius two. Direct journal access was intermittent (publisher fetch 403, author host certificate mismatch); the indexed author text and exact primary dissertation were checked. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2015.04.023).
- Kellerhals, Korenwein, Zschoche, Bredereck and Chen, *On the Computational Complexity of Variants of Combinatorial Voter Control in Elections*, arXiv:1701.05108, §4 Observation 1 and Appendix B.1, inherits NP-hardness for symmetric bundles of size at most three from the earlier paper. Full-radius permutation realizability is stronger than abstract symmetry. Their §4 Lemma 1 and Theorem 2 give constructive tractability on paths and cycles with **two candidates**. Their leader-budget parameterized hardness theorems in §3/§5 do not require full radius two. [Full author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.05108).

## Scope of the refinement

The proof realizes complete P5 components using explicit rankings and marker pairs, so it supplies the exact geometric restriction. For every fixed d≥2 the handoff’s block-replication or parity lift preserves the P5 components. Only that structural refinement transfers to the already settled d≥3 classification. An extra registered p-ballot yields a unique-winner variant while leaving the bundle graph unchanged. Neither extension is presented as a separate open problem.

Searches used the literal full-radius, size-three, plurality, two-swap and small-budget terms, then checked the thesis, earlier paper and the later symmetric-bundle paper. There is no claim about radius one with size at most three, hardness for constant k, single-crossing restrictions, or absolute historical priority.
