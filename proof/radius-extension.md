# Q364: larger fixed radii and unique winners

Let d≥2 be a fixed integer. For d=2 or d≥4 set t=floor(d/2) and replace each ranking by t consecutive copies on disjoint candidate labels, in a fixed block order. Between blocks all ballot pair orders agree, so a Kendall distance x becomes tx. As 2t≤d<3t (for those radii), exact radius-d adjacency is identical to the previous radius-two adjacency. For d=3 append two dummy candidates, their order being the parity of the old permutation relative to a reference. Their internal order disagrees between ballots iff x is odd; the new distance is x+(x mod 2), so the neighborhoods coincide. Preserve the score vector on the original candidate labels (block 1 in the replication case), applying the same lift to registered ballots. Auxiliary candidates get zero votes, and in the reduction the sentinel score is positive. The same fixed-size bundle restriction and disjoint induced five-vertex path graph therefore hold. Polynomial expansion for each fixed d. Radius one is not claimed. Previous larger-radius hardness is due to BCFNT Theorem 7; the refinement here is the same induced five-path restriction and common construction. See https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.6859.

Adding one registered ballot topped by p converts the co-winner condition to the unique-winner condition exactly, by integrality of plurality scores. Registered ballots do not enter bundle distances. Neither change requires retesting the original reduction's election outcomes, since the transformed election has the same collection of leader selections and comparison of original winning scores.
